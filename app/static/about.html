What this is

A way to ask what a kind of news event does to markets, and get an honest answer — including, most of the time, that there isn't one.

It holds real GDELT news events and real market prices in one graph. You can ask it two differently shaped questions, and they behave differently.

About a class of events — "what do US sanctions on China do?" It finds comparable past events, measures what each one was followed by, and aggregates. About one specific day — "what happened on 14 September 2019?" It measures that day against what the market normally does.

How to read the answer

The verdict box is the point of the whole thing. It says one of:

Detectable
The move is far outside what that market normally does. Single large shocks land here.
Below our bar
Something moved, but not far enough to separate from ordinary noise.
Not detectable
Nothing clears the bar, and the apparent signal fades as more evidence is added. Nearly every class question lands here.
Cannot be tested
Too few matching events to ask the question at all. Not a weak "no" — a statement that the question could not be put to the test.

"Not detectable" is the system working, not failing. Being able to tell an unanswerable question from an answerable one is the product.

What is in it

The two date ranges

The selector at the top of the app chooses which span of news a question is asked over.

The shorter one is pinned and does not move. Figures published in an earlier write-up were measured over exactly that span, and keeping it fixed is what lets anyone re-run those numbers and get the same answer.
